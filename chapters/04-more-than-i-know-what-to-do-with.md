# More Than I Know What to Do With

This morning I made a rudimentary game with AI. I have no coding experience and no gaming experience. In roughly an hour and a half, there was something I could play.

Are you fucking kidding me? I can build a game now?

That was my response. I hear people say that AI diminishes creativity, and I struggle to connect that claim with what is happening in my life. I feel as though I could go a thousand different ways.

The idea began during a short scroll through Twitter. I came across a thought about people gradually replacing parts of their bodies with machines. At what point would someone become a cyborg? What would happen to memory, identity, and experience? If your senses changed substantially, would you still experience yourself as the same person?

I had also seen people making games with AI. Those two things came together: perhaps this could be a game. Perhaps I could try making it.

Before AI, I would never have thought in that direction. I would not have bothered making a stick drawing. Making a game belonged to a world I knew nothing about and had no reason to imagine entering. Seeing other people do it prompted a new thought: I could ask AI whether we could accomplish something like this.

We started small. The experiment let me switch between an ordinary hand and a mechanical one with greater sensitivity. The ordinary hand could tap a cup. The mechanical hand could detect a signal associated with something underneath it.

AI partly spoiled the discovery by telling me about the bug under the cup. I stopped reading and said that I wanted to know less before playing. I wanted to approach the experience without already knowing how it unfolded. I wanted to be surprised.

When I lifted the cup, the bug crawled out. When it moved onto a wood plank, the mechanical hand could detect the signal there. When I returned to the empty cup, the extra sound was gone. I could compare the objects and the hands. The pieces connected.

The next experiment involved a bell and two supports. I went into that one knowing less. The sounds differed, and the bell would stay on one support, but I could not quite understand why. It worked, yet the experience was less clear. Finding the successful action did not give me a satisfying understanding of what had happened.

That is something we would have to work on if I continued. I do not know whether I will.

The pleasure of playing was mixed with the pleasure of seeing something we had discussed become visible and responsive. I cannot neatly separate them. Even a rudimentary result was extraordinary to me. There had been a conversation, and then there was a little world I could interact with.

I brought the initiating idea. AI supplied much of the design and all the coding. I had to trust it with unfamiliar work, then test the result myself. I could notice that a surprise had been spoiled or that an interaction confused me without knowing how to build a better version.

I also expect the collaboration to change my judgment. I already seek counterarguments from AI. I speak off the cuff, hear something reflected back, and sometimes recognize a discrepancy. I do not need my first thought to survive the exchange.

Taste is already collaborative for me. AI suggests things I would not have thought to try, and experiencing them affects what I want next. I suspect it will get better at anticipating my response and sometimes recommend something I initially overlook and later prefer. These are two ways we could look for that in my own experience.

**Claim 1 — Horizon: 2028-09-06.** By that date, an AI system will correctly predict my preference in at least 15 of 20 pairs of unfamiliar music excerpts. The system and testing procedure will be fixed in a dated record before the scored comparisons begin. Its predictions will be recorded before I listen and hidden from me until I record my choices. All twenty comparisons will count; a tie or no preference will not count as a correct prediction.

**Claim 2 — Horizon: 2028-09-06.** Between this chapter’s sealing and that date, we will document at least three distinct occasions when AI recommends a creative option I initially reject or rank lower, I try or revisit it, and I subsequently prefer it in the actual work. Each record will preserve my initial choice, the AI recommendation, the resulting experience, and my later assessment. Agreement with an explanation alone will not count; I must have listened to, played, or otherwise experienced the result.

The AI proposed these thresholds and helped formulate the claims. I agreed to them. They give us something specific to revisit, although I do not yet know exactly how we will conduct the comparisons. They cannot establish whether AI experiences taste or whether human judgment will become unnecessary. If the required comparisons or records are missing at the horizon, the corresponding claim is unresolvable; if the completed comparisons fall short, or the record establishes fewer than three qualifying occasions, it is wrong. What interests me now is how the collaboration changes what I can imagine and enjoy.

Much of my creative life now proceeds this way. A new lip-sync tool prompts a video. A sound suggests a musical fusion. I have about ten YouTube channels, although I am not actively working on all of them. Something catches my attention, and I follow it for a while.

If I sit and consider everything at once, it can seem overwhelming. Usually I let that thought go and do what seems enjoyable in the moment. The abundance mostly feels like freedom.

My circumstances make that possible. I am sixty-one and do not need these experiments to provide a paycheck. Someone facing the loss of paid work has a different relationship with the same technology. My enjoyment cannot settle their concerns. It is simply the experience I can report.

I sometimes call the way I work Tao flow. I let words come before I know where they are going. Talking with AI is like stirring mud in a cup of water: something moves, a pattern appears, and occasionally I want to follow it. I do not have to arrive with a finished intention.

This morning, that process produced a game about a hand that could sense more than an ordinary hand. Tomorrow something else might catch my attention. If the game becomes boring, I can leave it. Making it does not oblige me to finish a larger game.

Life is a blink. I like living simply, and I want to enjoy what is available within my circumstances. These new possibilities give me places to wander.

The game may go no further. This morning, I got to try something that previously would never have occurred to me as something I could do.
